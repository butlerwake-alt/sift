# SIFT | Follow the Money — concept specification (owner-provided, Nov 2026)
Mission: research-only intelligence engine that monitors public disclosures of capital allocation, tests whether those signals historically predicted returns, and explains when a signal has or has not worked. Help users understand evidence; never auto-mirror trades or promise profits.

1. Research streams: Congressional transactions (House/Senate disclosures: member, asset, range, transaction date, filing date); Institutional holdings (SEC 13F: position changes, concentration, multiple institutions entering same security); Corporate insiders (Form 4: separate open-market buys from grants, option exercises, 10b5-1 plans); Crypto and real-world adoption (verified treasuries, fund exposures, blockchain investments, corporate deployments; token demand vs. mere partnership announcements); Follow lists (follow people, firms, assets, categories; surface new records with source links). Owner also wants: big crypto wallet holders, and a top-10 list of social accounts with verifiable real track records.
2. Core signal engine: normalize identities/types/amounts/currencies/dates; detect clusters of independent actors accumulating the same asset within a public-information window; score source reliability, timeliness, conviction, breadth, market regime; separate facts, calculated signals, hypotheses, editorial; link each claim to primary evidence; show bullish and bearish evidence; mark unknowns.
3. Historical testing: congressional-copy baskets, insider open-market buy baskets, institutional accumulation, cross-source clustering, benchmarks; BTC, top-5 liquid crypto, S&P 500, individual securities. Point-in-time integrity (entry only after public disclosure + processing delay, never backdated). Predeclared regime definitions (bull/bear/sideways/high-vol). Metrics: cumulative/annualized return, max drawdown, volatility, win rate, trade count, turnover, benchmark-relative. Robustness: walk-forward/OOS, costs, slippage, survivorship, delistings, sensitivity, minimum samples. Fair comparisons; report negative/inconclusive results.
4. Visitor experience: daily dashboard of verified disclosures with publication-date labels; entity pages (politician, fund, insider, company, asset) with source-linked history; interactive performance charts by strategy/regime/period/benchmark; explainable signal cards (who, what, when public, why it matters, confidence, caveats); watchlists and research alerts; no trade execution.
5. Pipeline: official public sources (SEC EDGAR, congressional portals), honor terms and rate limits; store raw records, source URL, retrieval timestamp, filing date, transaction date, revisions, immutable snapshots; dedupe amendments, reconcile identities, log uncertainty, route ambiguous records for review; scheduled ingestion, reproducible backtests; publish freshness, coverage gaps, failures. Read-only: no wallets, brokerages, orders, purchases, or deployment without owner approval.
6. Phases: 1 Evidence foundation (source registry, schemas, ingestion prototypes, verified records, disclosure-delay handling); 2 Research dashboard; 3 Backtest lab; 4 Validation.
7. Success: every displayed result reproducible from stored source records; no lookahead leakage; state when a signal lacks statistical support; high historical return is not proof; nothing presented as guaranteed; disclosures are not real-time feeds.
8. For implementers: treat as a proposed module. First produce technical design, public-source inventory, data schema, timestamp model, regime definitions, backtest methodology, test plan, staged implementation proposal; identify legal/API limits and missing inputs. Preserve existing SIFT project, backups, protected branches, spending limits, owner approval gates. Do not modify or deploy live project without explicit authorization.
